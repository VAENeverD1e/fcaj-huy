> My personal reflections and feedback on the internship experience at the **First Cloud AI Journey (FCAJ)** program at **Amazon Web Services Viet Nam Company Limited (AWS Vietnam)**.

### Overall Evaluation

**1. Working Environment**  
The environment at AWS Vietnam is genuinely friendly and open — not the kind of corporate atmosphere where you feel afraid to ask questions. Everyone on the FCAJ team was approachable and happy to help whenever I got stuck. The workspace itself was comfortable and made it easy to focus. If I could suggest one thing, it would be having more cross-team bonding activities so interns can connect with people outside their immediate project group.

**2. Support from Mentor & Admin Team**  
My mentor was patient and explained things clearly without just handing me the answers. I appreciated being pushed to figure things out on my own first — that approach actually made me learn a lot faster. The admin team was responsive and handled all the logistical stuff smoothly so I could focus on the technical work.

**3. Relevance to My Major & Skill Alignment**  
My background is Computer Science (AWS082026 at Ho Chi Minh City University of Technology), and this project stretched my knowledge well beyond what I had studied. Getting to apply concepts from networking, operating systems, and distributed systems to real cloud-native security tools like CloudTrail, GuardDuty, and Elastic SIEM felt genuinely valuable — not like a typical intern "busywork" project.

**4. Learning & Skill Development**  
Beyond just the technical side, I picked up a lot about how real engineering projects are structured — writing documentation, thinking about cost and scalability from the start, reviewing your own code critically. The knowledge-sharing sessions with other participants also gave me exposure to architectures I would not have encountered otherwise.

**5. Company Culture & Team Spirit**  
AWS's Leadership Principles — especially *Learn and Be Curious* and *Bias for Action* — genuinely show up in how people work here. There is a culture of taking initiative and sharing knowledge rather than guarding it. Even as an intern, I felt like my contributions were taken seriously.

**6. Internship Policies & Benefits**  
The stipend was fair, the working arrangements were flexible, and having access to AWS training resources and workshops was a significant bonus. Being able to experiment with real AWS services during the internship rather than just studying theory made a huge difference.

---

### Key Takeaways & Feedback

#### 1. What I found most satisfying

Honestly, the moment everything clicked together. There is something uniquely satisfying about watching a custom KQL rule detect a simulated attack, seeing EventBridge fire the Step Functions orchestrator, Lambda auto-remediate the misconfiguration in under 10 seconds, and a DynamoDB record appear in the dashboard — all in one continuous flow that I built from scratch. That end-to-end feeling is what made all the debugging sessions worth it.

#### 2. What the program could improve for future interns

* **Peer review sessions**: Regular cross-project demos where interns present their architecture to each other would be really valuable. You learn a lot from seeing how someone else approached a completely different problem.

#### 3. Would I recommend this program?

**Yes, without hesitation — 10/10.** The FCAJ program gives you real technical autonomy, solid mentorship, and exposure to actual cloud architecture — not just lab exercises. If you are a student who wants to bridge the gap between academic knowledge and what cloud engineers actually do at work, this is one of the best ways to do it.

---

### Suggestions for the Future

* Introduce deep-dive workshops on advanced cloud security topics and AI-assisted threat detection — there is real appetite for that content among interns who want to go beyond foundational certifications.
